### F2R-01 · High · Two services register the same document through different controls

`EditorBridge.cs:57,79–103,121–122` checks the local open dictionary. `EditorCoordinator.cs:86–97` checks a foreign coordinator on the incoming **control**. Neither check establishes who already owns the incoming **document** outside this bridge. This is residual R1-01 across service instances. The desktop registers a singleton, so this reproduction requires a second in-process service and an editor binding. It does not require a second interpreter or a transport credential. [Bridge](../../CryptoWorkBenchAvalonia/Services/Automation/EditorBridge.cs) · [Coordinator](../../CryptoWorkBenchAvalonia/Services/Automation/EditorCoordinator.cs) · [Application registration](../../CryptoWorkBenchAvalonia/App.axaml.cs)

Bind A to service 1 and a different control B, with `B.Document = A.Document`, to service 2. Both coordinators reported their control as the current target. Input on A produced `Aa`, input on B produced `bAa`, and Undo on A reversed B's edit. Disposing service 2 restored ordinary input on A. The second binding should be denied before any independent registration acquires that buffer. Enforce unique live buffer ownership across supported service bindings, or enforce one service owner for editor registration while permitting independent authorized interpreter callers. Add the two-service alias, cross-Undo, rejection and later rebind as a regression. [Evidence, shared-buffer-two-services](../../artifacts/b1-f2-r1/counter.log)

## Architectural assessment and test boundaries

**Workspace and drain.** Within a single bridge, outgoing identity locks, updates, active operations and phase checks guard transition, then live document and stopping checks run after retirement. Retained activation preserves document identity and its undo stack. A third control claimed by another service during retirement was rejected, A remained activatable and its Undo worked. A throwing WorkspaceChanged callback was recorded and a normal replacement completed with subsequent input. Controlled multicast invocation can still skip later listeners when an earlier listener throws. F2R-02 shows why the ordering of the actual native finish notification matters. Genuine finish combined with a pending callback waited for both, including when the selected registration had closed. Certificates remain owner- and transition-bound. A native Changing exception during paste produced a faulted receipt, a closed group, zero pending tickets and refusal of subsequent native input. AP0 explicitly permits quarantine after uncertain native failure. F2R-05 is different because legitimate Cut geometry triggers that quarantine without any throwing external provider.

**Search and clipboard.** Replace All brackets complete enumeration with a monotonic policy witness, rechecks panel identity, options, source, document and active original ticket, then applies the frozen descending ranges. A deliberately dishonest provider which changed permissions while returning a constant revision was able to admit both matches. This establishes the trust boundary of the internal provider contract. It is not a defect in the monotonic check. Native and workbench results agreed for literal replacement of a regex capture spelling, overlapping candidates, zero-width matches, multiline matches, case folding, deletion, anchors and grouped Undo/Redo. Replace Next progression also agreed. Clipboard's post-acceptance plan prevented a changed selection or successor provider from redirecting the suffix paste. Of 16 extra ordinary/rectangular/whole-line Cut/Paste pairs with tabs, CRLF, anchor probes and optional partial protection, 15 agreed. F2R-11 explains the differing Undo step. The 45 additional rectangular paste pairs compared subsequent paste effects as well as geometry. Zero-width rectangular **Cut** with the whole-line option is the distinct F2R-05 case. [Original B1 replay](../../artifacts/b1-f2-r1/b1.log) · [F1-R1 replay](../../artifacts/b1-f2-r1/f1r1.log) · [Counterchecks](../../artifacts/b1-f2-r1/counter.log)

**Interpreter, Return and drafts.** The interpreter and host reservation are process-wide. Observer depth is thread-static and paired in finally. Nested same-thread observation that threw still denied another service's original request and permitted the same request after unwind. A deliberate Task.Run to another thread could submit while the originating thread was in observation. The documented restriction is synchronous and thread-local. This result does not establish asynchronous propagation or a general sandbox. Authorization and replay precede the fresh-work gate. Effectful finalization and state mutation advance the shared draft revision under reservation, while replies retain their service-local versions. A stale edit could not overwrite a foreign replacement, in-place write or effectful job. Capture and presentation for normal GUI execution remain within the held reservation. Invalid UI-only input causes the excessive invalidation in F2R-09. The private Return window handles ordinary revocation at injectable clock and registry boundaries, with the timeout gap in F2R-08. The five previously fixed findings B1-10, B1-11, B1-13, B1-14 and B1-15 retained their tested effects for replacement identity, parameter key, retired editor, Replace Next and detached Markdown respectively. **Completion and pinned native history.** The additional outer undo group defers size enforcement while optional recording is suppressed. F2 restores the saved limit in finally and adds the caret/selection operation on successful acceptance. At original limits one and two, two consecutive successful completion sessions matched plain AvaloniaEdit for text and caret after insertion, Undo and Redo. Disposal during the restoration notification also retained the executable original redo. In a deliberate raw-stack stress case a SizeLimit property listener immediately restored its own nonzero limit while F2 was suppressing recording. Later disposal prevented insertion but redo was lost. That probe writes the raw library history option directly. The AP0 host boundary excludes arbitrary external stack manipulation, so it is an observation, outside the 12 counted findings. F2 therefore depends on exclusive control of SizeLimit through startup and on the pinned optional-push and size-enforcement semantics. Keep near-limit histories, repeated sessions, throwing callbacks, the late protection check and actual executable redo as editor-upgrade gates.
